From Survival to Strategy

I became a single mother at 22 in the first year of my business degree. My entire childhood, I'd sometimes seen my parents briefly on weekday evenings between their work shifts. I looked at my newborn daughter and made a promise: she wouldn't grow up wondering where her mother was.

So I taught myself digital marketing in borrowed hours: late nights while my baby slept, early mornings before she woke. I started with babysitting to make ends meet, then moved into makeup artistry, hair salon management, and restaurant management. Each role taught me something different about customers, operations, and what makes businesses actually work. At 26, I launched Social Connect, a digital marketing consultancy built on everything I'd learned in those survival years.

One early client trusted me to market a large-scale cultural event, something that had never been positioned this way in Botswana before. I had no prior production experience. But I secured eight corporate sponsors, national media coverage, and celebrity participation by positioning an overlooked market as a premium opportunity.

Strategic clarity opened those doors, not experience, not budget, not connections. That moment revealed something I've built my entire practice around: strategic positioning opens doors that hustle alone never will. Clarity beats effort. Every time.

As I've grown and learned, Social Connect has expanded its services beyond digital marketing. What began as a survival strategy has become a platform for building legacy.

I work with people who are legacy-driven. Builders whose past didn't break them but refined them. They've experienced failure, disappointment, and pressure, yet still rise. For them, business isn't just about income; it's about impact, ownership, and building something that lasts beyond them.

These leaders understand the value of advice, the importance of implementation, and the necessity of investing in strategy and systems. They know paying for expertise accelerates growth. And many of them share something else in common: their children are watching.

The most powerful way to teach the next generation is to live the lesson. Building your dreams is not selfish; it is leadership. It shows what commitment, clarity, and courage look like in real time.

This belief is why my work is focused on Africa: partnering with African businesses and with global organizations ready to grow within the continent. Africa is not an emerging market. It is a growth frontier. And the leaders who will win here are those who build clarity, credibility, and infrastructure that compounds.
